# Lore

Draft. The core twist is decided. Characters and groups go by titles only: the Guild, the Creditor, the Old King, the Writ, the bearer.

## The twist

The citizen is the final boss.

- The citizen carries the soul of the Creditor, a thing that once ruled the kingdom. It was sealed into a commoner at birth.
- The Writ the citizen carries is not a letter. It is the seal.
- The Creditor's body lies at the bottom of the Hollow. The journey brings the soul back to it.
- At the bottom, the seal breaks and the Creditor takes the citizen's body. The player loses control.
- The mercs the player hired, equipped and trained fight the thing they were paid to protect. The player watches.

The whole run builds the party that has to beat the player.

## What the player believes

- The old capital sank into a chasm called the Hollow generations ago.
- A Blight rises out of the Hollow and spreads across the surface.
- The Guild of Contracts says a sealed Writ must be carried down to the sunken capital to stop it.
- The bearer is a commoner chosen by lot. The bearer cannot fight, so the Guild pays for mercenaries.
- Each run is a different bearer. The seed decides who.

## The truth

- The Guild of Contracts serves the Creditor. Each generation, it finds the new vessel and hires mercenaries to bring it down.
- The Old King did not fail. He sank the capital on purpose to bury the Creditor's body under it.
- The monsters are the Old King's soldiers and people. They are cursed, but they still stand guard. That is why many of them ignore the mercs and go for the citizen.
- The Blight is the Hollow trying to keep the body down. It is not attacking the surface.
- The monster compendium is a list of people who died trying to stop the player.

### Who the party fights

Every enemy in the Hollow has a reason to stop the bearer. "Monsters" is only what the party calls them.

The Creditor has no servants in the Hollow. Its only agents are on the surface: the Guild, and the contracts the mercs signed. Nothing that spawns helps the party, and nothing that spawns wants the bearer to reach the tomb. The only exception is the final fight, once the Creditor has woken: then it takes corrupted mercs and raises unresurrected ones.

| Enemy | Who they are | Why they attack |
|---|---|---|
| Hollow soldiers | The Old King's soldiers, bound by his curse to guard the tomb forever. | Duty. They know what the bearer carries. |
| Penitents | Members of the Church, the kingdom's religion. See [the Church](#the-church). | They keep the seal, and the bearer is what it holds back. |
| Blight creatures | The Hollow's own flesh, grown to keep the Creditor's body down. | Like an immune response, they attack what does not belong. |
| The Hollowed | Penitents driven mad by the Creditor's whispers. See [the Church](#the-church). | Hunger. They want to tear the bearer apart and take what it carries. |

### The Church

- The Church was the kingdom's religion, and the Old King was its protector.
- When the King sank the capital, the Church sealed the tomb with blood rites. Its most faithful stayed below to keep the seal: the Penitents.
- The blood sigils, candles and altars in the Hollow are sealing rites, not summonings. The gore is real, and it is done to keep something worse shut.

The Church did not stay pure. Centuries underground, beside the body, wore it down in two ways:

- **The Penitents became fanatics.** The seal needs fresh blood, and the rites grew from symbolic to real. They take people from the surface for it. The bodies on the gibbets and the heads on pikes are their offerings. They are right to stop the bearer, and they are horrifying.
- **Some went mad: the Hollowed.** They listened too long to the Creditor's whispers. The whispers gave them no loyalty, only hunger. They are drawn to the Writ and want to tear the bearer apart for it. Their faith is gone, and they are Occult now, so Radiant attacks hurt them.
- The eye on their banners is the Church's: the faithful watching over the seal.
- Their faith is Radiant. The party's own Cleric shares it, and Radiant attacks deal nothing to Penitents, who are Radiant themselves. The holy magic the party relies on refuses to hurt the Church.

## Why the citizen keeps going deeper

The journey goes down. Each phase is a layer of the Hollow.

| Phase | Layer | Biome |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Marches | The surface near the rim: forest roads, burned villages. |
| 2 | The Rift Road | Paths cut into the chasm walls. |
| 3 | The Drowned Undercroft | Flooded caverns and old cellars under the city. |
| 4 | The Sunken Crown | The ruins of the old capital. |

Reasons to go deeper:

1. **The pull.** The citizen feels drawn downward. Their dreams grow clearer with each layer.
2. **The Blight.** The Guild says it rises every day and the surface will fall if the Writ does not arrive.
3. **No way back.** Paths collapse behind the party. Turning back voids the contract, and the mercs leave.
4. **The Guild's pay.** Wages are paid only at Waystations, the Guild posts at the top of each layer. The vault is the Guild's strongbox there.
5. **A promised reward.** The Guild promises each background something: a pardon, a fortune, a title, a cure. Every promise is a lie.
6. **Earlier bearers.** The party finds their remains on the way down, each holding the same Writ.

## Clues along the way

Each clue is true from the start. The player only understands them at the end.

- The Cleric's holy magic does nothing to Penitents.
- The Church's eye banners hang along the whole descent, as if something has been watching for the bearer.

- Many monsters ignore the mercs and go only for the citizen.
- Dead mercs do not rot. Their corpses wait. The contract holds their souls.
- The Writ grows warm near Occult altars.
- Occult corruption spreads to mercs who stay close to the citizen.
- Every dead bearer carries the same Writ, word for word. Only the name differs.
- The Stranger, who appears across runs, warns the party not to reach the bottom.
- Some events let the citizen read part of the Writ. The text does not make sense until the end.
- Shrines are the Creditor's altars. Accepting a shrine's curse feeds it. See [the Creditor's strength](#the-creditors-strength).

## The final fight

### The reveal

- The last node of the Sunken Crown is the Creditor's tomb.
- The seal breaks. The citizen becomes the Creditor.
- From this point the player cannot move or act. The Creditor is run by the AI.
- The player can still click a merc to inspect it, as at any time in the game. Nothing else.
- The mercs fight on their own, as they always have, with the gear, cards, skills and toggles the player gave them.

### Who fights on which side

The sides come from systems the player managed during the run.

| State at the reveal | Effect |
|---|---|
| Living merc | Fights the Creditor. |
| Dead merc, not resurrected | The contract still holds its soul. The Creditor raises it as a servant. |
| Corrupted merc | The Creditor takes control of it. It fights for the Creditor. |
| Cowardly merc with low morale | May flee from the fight. |
| The Old King's ghost | Joins the mercs if the party carries at least one piece of his regalia. See [the Old King](#the-old-king). |

- What the citizen carried still matters. The Creditor uses the consumables in the citizen's pack, such as bombs and potions.
- Skills and toggles that refer to the citizen stop working. Interpose, Vow and Aegis have nothing to protect. Stances that kept mercs close to the citizen now put them next to the Creditor.

### The Creditor's strength

The Creditor grows from souls the run fed it. Every source is a choice the player made, so the scaling adds risk to decisions instead of punishing normal play.

| Source | Souls | The choice behind it |
|---|---|---|
| A merc dies, even if it is resurrected later | 1 per death | Taking risky fights, leaving mercs exposed. |
| A merc stays dead at the reveal | Raised as a servant (see the sides above) | Not paying to resurrect it. |
| A shrine's curse is accepted | 1 per curse | Taking a shrine's power with its curse. |
| A Pilgrim uses a shrine | 1 per shrine | The Pilgrim never sees a curse, but each shrine still feeds the Creditor. The most devout bearer feeds it the most. |
| A Cursed item is worn at the reveal | 1 per item | Keeping a strong Cursed item instead of cleansing it. |

- Each soul raises the Creditor's health and damage by a set amount, and some thresholds add skills.
- The Writ shows the count without explaining it. It grows warmer and heavier as souls are added. The citizen's weight limit drops by a little for each soul.
- On a first run this is a mystery. On later runs it is a budget the player manages on purpose.

### The Old King

The Old King sank the capital to bury the Creditor. His ghost still guards the tomb. He is the only ally the party has in the final fight, and he has to be earned.

His regalia lies scattered across the Hollow:

| Piece | Where | What it gives in the final fight |
|---|---|---|
| The Signet | An event or elite in phase 2 | The ghost appears. At the start of the fight he binds the Creditor for a few seconds. |
| The Scepter | An event or elite in phase 3 | Every 30 seconds he casts a seal that stuns the Creditor. Mercs deal more damage to it while it is sealed. |
| The Crown | An elite or hidden node in phase 4 | He frees one merc the Creditor has taken over, returning it to the party's side. |

- Each piece is a heavy item in the citizen's pack. Carrying it costs weight for the whole journey.
- Thieves can steal a piece. The vault keeps it safe only between phases.
- Each piece adds a compendium entry with part of his story.
- With no pieces, the ghost does not appear and the mercs fight alone.
- The ghost cannot be killed. The Creditor can banish him for a while, which pauses his seals.

### Endings

- **The mercs win.** The Creditor is sealed again, and the bearer dies with it. The run is cleared. Losing the player's own body is the good ending.
- **The mercs lose.** The Creditor rises. The run is lost, and this is the dark ending.
- The compendium records which ending each run reached.

### First run and later runs

- On the first run the twist is a surprise. The player has prepared for the wrong fight.
- On later runs the player knows. The compendium shows the Creditor once it has been beaten or has risen.
- A player who knows will prepare on purpose: resurrect every dead merc, cleanse corruption, bring Radiant damage, and pay wages so morale holds.
